# Act VI: Beneath the Ice (proposal, story-first)

«Πράξη VI: Κάτω από τον Πάγο» / "Act VI: Beneath the Ice". One of the competing proposals for the owner's chosen act; this one is written from the story outward. It answers the line Act V left in the post-game, Alkyone's d.alk.gates: «Οι πύλες σας δεν έχουν πάτο, λέτε; Έχουν. Είναι κάτω από τον πάγο μας.» / "Your gates have no bottom, you say? They do. It's under our ice."

Act VI begins from a save at quest 31 (`seaLit`, `h.act5 >= 0`): the Skotos is named and has sunk, the Farthest Light burns, Whitecliff sees three sea-lights flash on its northern horizon, and a village child says «Αλιγενείς. Το θυμάμαι.» / "Saltborn. I remember." at the beacon.

**The act in one paragraph.** It is the halcyon days («οι αλκυονίδες»), the still weeks in the heart of winter when the sea is quiet. In Whitecliff people begin to forget small things: the child forgets the Saltborn's name, Halda (a Stoneborn, who "don't forget") forgets what she meant to say about the stars, and Elianthe forgets who planted the staff by the beacon. Frost grows inside Isarn's dark lantern. Tern, Alkyone's ten-year-old son, walks south with Tamarisk to find the beacon-keeper. His mother saw a light flicker far below the clear ice where the Skotos sank, cut through the ice and went down after it. The ice closed over her. Down there she is forgetting, and Tern is afraid she will forget him. He knows every name, "yours too", and he knows what she told him: the beacon-keeper's gates have a bottom. The hero takes the Shadow Gate with no number down through the dark's dreams to the world beneath the sea ice. There they relight the four Drowned Lights, kill the Ketos (the old beast whose steady light has drawn boats under on moonless nights), and go down to the Nethermost. At the bottom a woman of clear ice kneels with her hand in the black water, and a hand as big as a boat holds hers. She is the First, whose name the sea took.

**The villain.** Κήυκας ο Αλησμόνητος / Keyx the Unforgotten: Alkyone's husband, Tern's father, the fisherman who "rowed out one moonless night and did not come back" (d.stone.keyx). He did not drown. Every night his wife said his name, so the dark could not take him and the sea could not keep him. For seven winters he hung beneath the ice, held, and in the dark he found the Skotos's other hand and held it. On the Night Without Fires he opened his hand, and that is why the Skotos rose in Act V. Now he means to make the First let go too, so that the dark rises up the Shadow Gates, the whole North forgets, and everyone, living and dead, can rest. His argument is the Skotos's own offer with a human face, and he takes Isarn's last word for his banner: «Ξεκουράσου.»

**The twists, in three blows.**
1. **The voice below is real.** The game has trained the player to distrust a beloved voice from the dark: Karthax spoke to Isarn in Ivar's voice. The light Alkyone followed did flicker, so it was fire, and the voice after the Ketos falls is really Keyx's. It also speaks in capital letters, not the Skotos's lowercase. The lure that does not flicker is a separate danger, and the act teaches the difference: «Ποτέ μην τραβάς κουπί προς φως που δεν τρέμει.» / "Never row toward a light that doesn't flicker."
2. **Remembering can be a cage.** Act V's tenderest line, d.alk.b5 «Ακόμα λέω τ' όνομά του. Γι' αυτό είναι ακόμα δικός μου.» / "I still say his name. That's why he's still mine.", turns out to be the game's oldest rule: every gift has its cage (altar.sub). Amaranthe wished that nothing of hers would fade. Alkyone has done the same to Keyx without a shard.
3. **The rite was a lullaby, and the Gates are the dark's dreams.** The First did not lose her name to the sea. She went down after the dark empty-handed, because it was weeping like a child sent away from the fire, and she gave it her hand. Her people said the names every night so she would know she was not forgotten, and said its name last "so it would know it was ours as well". Over the ages they forgot why, and came to say it "so it cannot hide" (d.glaukos.r2). The Skotos sleeps and dreams of everything it took: barrows, woods, fears, the first ones first (that is why the Gate Guardian is always an Act I foe). Its dreams rise into the North as the Shadow Gates. They have no bottom because dreams have none. They do have a floor: a hand held at the bottom of the sea.

**The ending.** Keyx is beaten but not killed. Tern, who has just made his forgetting mother remember him by telling her every name, his own and the hero's among them, walks to the edge of the black water and says «Καληνύχτα, Σκότος.» / "Good night, Skotos." The dark answers in lowercase («...καληνύχτα...») and falls asleep. The First lets go at last. Her ice turns to water "and the water stays water" (the Saltborn creed, d.ice.i1.3). Before it does, she tells Tern her name, which the player never hears. Alkyone lets Keyx go: «Θα λέω τ' όνομά σου κάθε βράδυ. Όχι για να σε κρατήσω. Για να σου πω καληνύχτα.» / "I'll say your name every night. Not to hold you. To say good night." He walks into the black water, and a small blue bird flies low over it and is gone. Selna says their names from above, and the ice opens for whoever comes up with a name. At home the hero hangs Tern's lamp on Isarn's staff, beside the lantern that "went out quietly, as if falling asleep" (d.answer.0). One lantern sleeps and one burns. Then, as on the night before the game began, «Κοίτα βόρεια.» / "Look north.": on the six dark peaks fires catch, one after another, lit by hands you know. Every watcher in this game kept watch alone: Isarn at ten (d.lamp.m5.2 «Μη με αφήσεις μόνο μαζί του.»), Arna, Ivar, the Lampless, the Saltborn, the First. Act VI ends the lonely watch. The last line is «Ο Βορράς κοιμάται με τα φώτα αναμμένα.» / "The North sleeps with the lights on."

**Why this story is the right one** (the owner's brief, point by point):
- **The beacons.** intro.3 «Όσο καίνε, ο Βορράς είναι ασφαλής.» comes true a third time. Act IV: the crown-fire. Act V: the remembering that held the dark down. Act VI: night-lights for a dark that sleeps. The seven peaks of d.isarn.2 burn again, lit by hand.
- **Isarn.** He is not below, because «όσους θυμάστε, δεν κατεβαίνουν ποτέ εδώ» / "those you remember never come down here". His sleeping lantern is why the Gates open at his staff. His word «Ξεκουράσου.» is argued over by the villain and the First, and the First wins: «Η ανάπαυση είναι να κοιμάσαι κι άλλος να κρατά το φως.» / "Rest is sleeping while someone else keeps the light." (cf. Selna's d.selna.rest).
- **Arna.** His founding words «Κάποιος πρέπει να φυλάει και την πύλη» (d.lamp.m4.2) are answered: someone has to keep watch, only it need not be one.
- **Karthax and the Ash Crown.** Isarn's last request is fulfilled on screen. Deep among the forgotten, a dying ember in the ice asks the hero what its name was, and «Δεν του λες τίποτα.» / "You tell it nothing." It goes out "like red-hot iron dropped in water", the image of the Breaking (d.tear.breaking.1). His name is never said, and nothing of him fights.
- **Selna and the Saltborn.** The nightly rite gets its first meaning back. Glaukos's nine lights are all lit at last. Selna names the hero.
- **The Skotos named.** Naming gave it an "I" (d.skotos.die2). A thing with an "I" can be argued with (Keyx) and can be wished good night (Tern).
- **The Shadow Gates.** They are explained and still play exactly as they do, and they gain one small story verb (see Echoes and post-game).

**Scope** (the Act V frame, smaller in engine, larger in story):
- **2 zones:** `under` «Κάτω από τον Πάγο» / "Beneath the Ice" (level 34) and `floor` «Ο Πάτος» / "The Nethermost" (level 37). They continue the +3 cadence of engine brief §4.
- **7 regular enemy entries (8 kinds):**

  - the Hull-louse as the Brooder's brood.
- **2 bosses:** «Το Κήτος» / "The Ketos" (Sketchfab "Pistosaur Animated" with a lure built in code) and «Κήυκας ο Αλησμόνητος» / "Keyx the Unforgotten" (a people recipe, with the shipped Hand of the Skotos at ×4 as the arm he holds).
- **Quests 32 to 38; the end state is 39.** Seven steps, as in Act V.
- **3 mechanics:**
  - Forgetting («Λήθη»), which touches the progression's four-button loadout;
  - True and False Light, with black water that light turns back into water (the creed made playable);
  - Names from Above (Selna's rite falling through the ice as a timing window).
- **World-state:** `tern6` (the forgetting at home), then `descent`, `boatLit`, `drowned0`-`3`, `ketosDown`, `alkBack`/`keyxDown`, `asleep`, `upThrough`, `lampHung` (the seven peaks).
- **Also:**
  - four optional Forgotten to free («Οι Ξεχασμένοι»): Leif, the woodcutter's son from Act I's d.v.3; the Sailor from Act V's Name-stone; Asger of the Order of the Flame (the Mage's thread); Kymo the twin;

- **Budget:** about 3.2 MB raw new and about 33 MB of new phone texture. The act leans on the shipped `rime` pack, the `frost` people set and the `act5` creatures, so a cold start into Act VI stays near 100 MB, under the 110 MB bar (itemised under Assets).

**The look in one idea: the ice is the sky.** Under the sea ice the ceiling glows faint green-white from the aurora above. The gameplay camera never sees it, so, as in Act V, the sky goes on the floor:
- black-water pools mirror the ceiling's glow;
- the Farthest Light's beam, turning above the ice, crosses the floor every 12 s as a moving band of real light;
- the four Drowned Lights rise from the sea floor into the ice like pillars holding up the sky.

The ice ceiling itself is drawn only in cinematics, which lower the camera.

## Story
### premise
**At quest 31.** Act V left the North at its happiest. The dark has a name (game.sub5), the sea-lights answer down the coast, a child in Whitecliff tells everyone «Αλιγενείς», and every night at the Farthest Light Selna says the names into the dark: the First, Einar, Arna, every Name-stone the hero lit, Ivar, Isarn, and last «Σκότος.» Act V also left four threads open:
- **Alkyone's husband.** «Κήυκας. Βγήκε με τη βάρκα του μια νύχτα χωρίς φεγγάρι και δεν γύρισε. Η Αλκυόνη λέει ακόμα τ' όνομά του.» (d.stone.keyx), and «...Γι' αυτό είναι ακόμα δικός μου.» (d.alk.b5).
- **The First.** «Η Πρώτη, που τ' όνομά της το πήρε η θάλασσα.» (d.glaukos.r1). Ice Memory 1 ends with the dark under the sea, "waiting for them to forget", and says nothing of where the woman went.
- **The Gates' bottom.** d.alk.gates says the Shadow Gates have one, "under our ice".
- **Tern's knowledge.** «Τα ξέρω ήδη όλα. Και το δικό σου.» / "I know them all already. Yours too." (d.tern.again). A ten-year-old Saltborn boy knows the hero's name, which the game has never printed.

Act VI pulls all four threads at once.

**The halcyon days.** The act opens on a narrated line over Whitecliff (d.hal.1): «Στην καρδιά του χειμώνα έρχονται οι αλκυονίδες: μέρες που η θάλασσα σωπαίνει και δεν φυσάει.» / "In the heart of winter come the halcyon days: days when the sea is still and no wind blows." The Greek phrase comes from the old tale of Alkyone and Keyx, the wife and the drowned husband who became sea-birds. The game never tells that tale; it lets the names and the season rhyme with it. The calm is the dark lying still under the ice, listening.

**What has happened below** (revealed over the act, never in one speech):
- **Seven winters ago** (Tern was three), Keyx rowed toward a steady light on a moonless night. It was the Ketos's lure. The Ketos took the boat. Keyx did not drown: every night Alkyone said his name, and in the house's rule a name said every night is not taken by the dark (boon.name.q «Ένα όνομα που λέγεται κάθε βράδυ δεν το παίρνει το σκοτάδι.»). He hung beneath the ice, called home every night and unable to come.
- **In the dark he felt for something to hold.** He found a hand as big as a boat and held it, the way the First holds the other: «Δεν είσαι μόνο σου. Ούτε εγώ.» / "You're not alone. Nor am I."
- **On the Night Without Fires** (d.u.night) the hand in his tightened and wanted to rise. Keyx, tired past bearing, opened his hand: «Πήγαινε. Ας ξεχάσουν. Ας ξεκουραστούμε.» / "Go. Let them forget. Let us rest." That is why, after a thousand years, "it rose" (d.alk.t1). Alkyone's explanation in Act V (the tyrant's remembering fire was gone) stays true; this is the hand that let go.
- **After the naming** the Skotos sank, and it now has an "I" (d.skotos.die2 «...ναι... αυτό... είμαι...»). A thing with an "I" can listen. Keyx talks to it. He has walked its roots for years and knows the Shadow Gates are its dreams. Now he means to make the First let go too, so that it rises up every Gate at once and the whole North forgets. The forgetting at Whitecliff is the first of it, coming up through the Gate at Isarn's staff.
- **Alkyone**, keeping the nightly watch at the Farthest Light, saw through the clear ice of the Skotos's sealed hole (Act V: "The hole freezes clear") a light far below that **flickered**. She knew Keyx's lantern. She cut through and went down. «Ο πάγος κλείνει πάνω από ό,τι κατεβαίνει.» / "The ice closes over whatever goes down." Below she found him, took his hand, and is forgetting everything else.

**The hook.** Tern and Tamarisk come to Whitecliff. Before Alkyone went down she told Tern what she had told the hero (d.alk.gates): if she did not come back, the southerners' gates have a bottom, under our ice. Tamarisk brings him because a boy should not walk the Field of Ash alone. Her Act V farewell to the Ranger was «Έλα να με βρίσκεις, αδελφή.» (d.tam.k.r); now she is the one who came.

**Why the North forgets now, and why at home.** The Shadow Gates are the sleeping dark's dreams, rising wherever the North has forgotten something (d.isarn.r5: they opened "as the fires failed"). Since Act IV, the nearest door to Whitecliff is Isarn's lantern, which "went out quietly, as if falling asleep" (d.answer.0). A sleeping thing opens a door into dreams. As Keyx pulls the dark upward, forgetting comes out of that door first, and Whitecliff, the place that taught the North to remember the Saltborn, is the first to lose the name.

### villain
**Κήυκας ο Αλησμόνητος / Keyx the Unforgotten.** The epithet's form is Amaranthe's («Αμαράνθη η Αμάραντη», no comma), and on purpose: both are caged by "nothing must fade". Boss tagline mon.keyx.t «Τον φώναζαν κάθε βράδυ, κι έτσι δεν έφυγε ποτέ» / "They called him every night, so he never left".

**Who he is.** A Saltborn fisherman and keeper, about fifty, salt-white beard. His oilskin has rotted to kelp. He trails a net cloak heavy with forgotten things he has gathered in the dark: lanterns, a child's shoe, oars, bones. In one hand is his own lantern, true fire that still flickers after seven winters below (it is what Alkyone saw). With the other he holds the dark's second Hand, a black arm rising out of the abyss behind him that moves as he moves, like a puppeteer whose puppet is bigger than the stage. He is not a monster. He is a man holding a monster's hand.

**What he wants.** Rest, for everyone. He has seen what the dark keeps: the forgotten of every age wandering its dreams, the First worn to almost nothing, himself woken every night by a voice he loves. His conclusion is the Skotos's offer from Act V («...ξεκουράσου... αυτό δεν σου είπε;...», d.skotos.w4), made by a man, out of love:
- «Αν ξεχάσουν όλοι, δεν θα πονάει κανείς. Ούτε εσύ.» / "If everyone forgets, no one will hurt. Not even you."

**Why he is a strong villain.** He is half right, and the game has said so before. Amaranthe's "let nothing of ours fade" was a cage (Act III), and the First Autumn freed the wood: «Θα γεράσουμε, όσοι μείναμε. Επιτέλους.» (d.elati.aut3). Keyx is that lesson pushed too far: if holding on is a cage, he wants to let go of everything. The answer, the First's, is in the middle: remember the name, release the person, keep a light.

**His voice.** Folk register: plain, dry and salty like Alkyone's, with contractions in English. He counts in winters. He speaks in the first person in normal case, never the Skotos's lowercase. That is how the player knows the voice after the Ketos is a man and not the dark. One climactic line in capitals, in phase 3. He calls the hero «φρυκτωρέ» like his wife, and Tern «Γλαρόνι».

**What he never does.** He grants no wish (no shard is left), never names the tyrant, and is never killed by the hero. He is beaten, then let go.

**The dark itself.** Το Σκότος stays what Act V made it: no wish, no plan. "It doesn't hate. It doesn't want. It forgets." (d.alk.b4). Two things are new:
- since its naming it has an "I", and lowercase lines that say «μου» and «φοβάμαι»;
- the First's reading of it: «Δεν είναι κακό. Είναι μόνο του. Τα μοναχικά πράγματα ξεχνούν.» / "It is not evil. It is alone. Lonely things forget."

It still drinks light, freezes and makes the world forget. It is pulled toward rising, and it is afraid of fire, "a little". It is not fought in Act VI. It is put to sleep.

### twist
Three blows, each built from shipped lines, at three points in the act.

**1. The voice below is real (q35, at the Ketos's death).**
- The set-up, in reverse. Act IV's great deception was a beloved voice from the flame: «η φωνή του μου μιλούσε μέσα από τη φλόγα» (d.isarn.e3). Players will expect the voice under the ice to be the dark wearing Keyx.
- The rule, taught at the hook and in Black-Water Memory 1: a light that does not flicker is a lure. Alkyone said the light below flickered.
- The proof, at the Black Mere. A lantern hangs on a rock in the black water. Tern: «Δεν είναι της μάνας μου. Δεν τρέμει.» / "That's not Mother's. It doesn't flicker." The rock rises: it is the Ketos's head.
- Then, when the Ketos falls, a man's voice comes up from below, in normal case: d.keyx.v1 «Αλκυόνη; ...Όχι. Ποιος είναι εκεί πάνω με τη φωτιά;» / "Alkyone? ...No. Who's up there with the fire?" Tern: d.tern6.k5 «...Μπαμπά;» / "...Dad?" Earlier, at the Upturned Boat, Tern said he does not remember his father's face, only his voice when he sang at the oars (d.tern6.c2).

**2. Remembering can be a cage, and Act V's rising was a hand that let go (q34 memories, q36).**
- Black-Water Memory 2: «Κάθε φορά που ακούει τ' όνομά του, ξυπνάει.» / "Every time he hears his name, he wakes."
- Black-Water Memory 3: on the Night Without Fires, «Ο ψαράς ανοίγει το χέρι του.» / "The fisherman opens his hand."
- At the bottom Keyx says it himself: d.keyx.a1 «Κι εγώ κρατούσα το άλλο του χέρι, εφτά χειμώνες, χωρίς να το ξέρω. Όταν έσβησαν οι φωτιές σας, άνοιξα το χέρι μου.» / "And I held its other hand, seven winters, without knowing. When your fires went out, I opened my hand." Then d.keyx.a2 «Ανέβηκε. Το είδατε.» / "It rose. You saw it."
- d.alk.b5 "That's why he's still mine" now reads as the act's cage. The house's oldest rule, «κάθε δώρο έχει το κλουβί του», needs no shard.

**3. The rite was a lullaby; the Gates are its dreams; the bottom is a hand (q34 Memory 4, q36 at the bottom).**
- **Black-Water Memory 4** continues Ice Memory 1 from the other side. The woman leaves the fire to the others and goes down, empty-handed, after something weeping "like a child sent away from the fire".
- **The First's own words** at the bottom (d.first.a1-a10, beats q36):
  - she went down and gave it her hand;
  - her people said the names so she would know she was not forgotten, and its name last "so it would know it was ours as well";
  - over the ages they forgot why, and now say it "so it cannot hide";
  - it sleeps and dreams of all it took, and its dreams rise as gates: «Γι' αυτό δεν έχουν πάτο. Ο πάτος είμαι εγώ.» / "That is why they have no bottom. The bottom is me."
- **The Ranger's mirror.** The dark, sent away from the fire as a child, mirrors the Evergreen children sent "to the edges of the North" (d.tear.nursery.1). The Skotos says so to the Ranger alone (d.skotos6.w5.r).

**Nothing is retconned.**
- d.glaukos.r2 «...για να μην κρυφτεί.» is what the Saltborn believe. It is a later reading of an older rite, which is how the game has always treated history: incomplete, not false (intro.3 "so no one would forget" was Karthax's wish working through his fire).
- Act V's explanation of the rising (d.alk.t2-t5) stays true: Keyx's open hand is a second cause, not a replacement.
- The Skotos is still not Karthax's servant, jailer or creation (canon rule 2). Karthax never knew of it, the First, or Keyx.
- Every φρυκτωρία is still the brow-stone's fire (d.isarn.e1). The six peaks relit at the end are hand-lit beacons, the way Act IV relit the four.
- Isarn and Ivar stay dead and are not below: "those you remember never come down here".
- Selna's name, saved and remembered in Act V, is not touched. Alkyone is never written out of the save. She is forgetting, not forgotten.
- The Gates play exactly as they do. The Gate Guardian is still a Barrow Lord, a Weaver or a troll, and now there is a reason: «Ονειρεύεται τους φόβους σας. Τους πρώτους πρώτα.» / "It dreams your fears. The first ones first."

**Old lines that read differently afterwards** (no line changes):
- d.answer.0 "the lantern's flame goes out quietly, as if falling asleep": Isarn's lantern sleeps, which is why it is a door.
- d.staff «Ξεκουράσου.»: a good-night.
- intro.4 «Απόψε σβήνουν, η μία μετά την άλλη.» → d.peaks.2 «Στις κορυφές πιάνουν φωτιές, η μία μετά την άλλη.»
- d.answer.4 «Κανείς που ξέρουμε δεν την άναψε.» → d.peaks.3 «Τις ανάβουν χέρια που ξέρεις.»
- d.isarn.2 «Κοίτα βόρεια. Πριν από τρεις νύχτες έκαιγαν εφτά φωτιές...» → «Κοίτα βόρεια.» and seven fires.
- d.lamp.m5.2 (Isarn at ten) «Μη με αφήσεις μόνο μαζί του.» → Tern, ten, in the Keyx fight: «Μη μ' αφήσεις μόνο!», and the hero does not.
- diff.skotos.d «Εδώ ξεχνιούνται και οι ήρωες.» / "Here even heroes are forgotten." → Alkyone forgets the hero, and a child remembers them.
- d.karthax.die «Ποιος... ποιος θα με θυμάται;» → the ember in the ice: «...πώς με έλεγαν;...» No one tells it.

### ending
**The good night** (cine `goodnight`; flags `keyxDown`, `alkBack` and `asleep` saved first; about 30 s, skippable after the first view):
- Keyx, on his knees, his Hand sinking: d.keyx.die0 «...Δεν ήθελα να πονάει κανείς.» / "...I didn't want anyone to hurt."
- Tern walks to the edge of the black water, where the First's hand is held, and says to the dark: d.tern6.g1 «Καληνύχτα, Σκότος.» / "Good night, Skotos."
- The Skotos (pale lowercase `say` over a low, slow ice-song): d.skotos6.g1 «...καληνύχτα...» / "...good night..."
- The great Hand loosens and lies still on the water. The abyss goes from moving black to black glass. Beneath it, for the first time in the game, the Skotos's whole shape can be seen asleep (a dark outline under the surface).
- The First: d.first.g1 «Επιτέλους κοιμάται. Τόσο καιρό περίμενα κάποιον να του το πει.» / "At last it sleeps. So long I waited for someone to say it."
- Alkyone, to Keyx: d.alk.g6 «Κήυκα. Θα λέω τ' όνομά σου κάθε βράδυ. Όχι για να σε κρατήσω. Για να σου πω καληνύχτα.» / "Keyx. I'll say your name every night. Not to hold you. To say good night."

## Bosses
Both fights keep Act V's phone rules:
- **At most two big telegraphs are ever up at once,** and an environment warning (a Forgetting Wave, the Mere draining, a Smother at a lamp) counts as one.
- **Telegraph durations never change with difficulty.**
- **Every phase has one thing to *do* besides hitting,** and an ally says it once.
- **Exactly one boss actor per fight.** The lure stalk, the net weights and Keyx's Hand are 'node' actors; Tern, Tamarisk, Alkyone and the First are NPCs.

- **Shipped scale.** The integrator re-tuned Act V's bosses to hp 170 each (data.js), so Act VI starts just above them.
